## The three layers and their jobs — sextant · map · projection (they are NOT competitors)

The geodesy triad names them exactly. A **sextant** takes a sighting and reports position *with its own error
bar*. A **map** renders the frame for use, distorting deliberately (Mercator inflates the poles on purpose). A
**projection/datum** is useful not because it is *true* but because it is *shared and its error is bounded*.

| Layer | Geodesy role | Is | Job | Judged by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Datum** | the **projection** | the frozen *form*: κ=5/4, n=2, quartet objective, two anchors, tokenizer, GTDB tree | the shared bounded frame everything registers against | stability, minimality, theorem-consistency |
| **Codec** (dim-16) | the **sextant** | the minimal, low-bias witness | report (position, **confidence**); make the map's distortion measurable | cross-instrument reproducibility |
| **v9 atlas** (dim-129) | the **map** | the operational realization | place genomes at resolution (stays live); its distortion is *characterized* | placement accuracy |

The codec is **not served** and **does not compete with v9 on placement** — a sextant does not lose to a survey
drone; it takes the sighting the drone is checked against. Its value is *evidentiary*: the codec↔atlas residual
**is** the map's label-distortion (measured, +0.087 std). It earns its keep as the ruler, not as a rival placer.

## What each layer is NOT (identity, not status)

Roles are defined as much by refusal as by capability. These are *identity* statements — for what is
verified vs open (with numbers), see the ledger of record, [`EVIDENCE.md`](EVIDENCE.md).

- The **codec/sextant** is **not** a function predictor, and **not** a better placer than v9 (0.567 vs 0.617
  family). Those are not failures — a datum-witness *must* be minimal; a frame that absorbed function or
  chased placement would stop being a stable, low-bias reference. Its poverty is the point.
- The **atlas/map** is **not** the frame. Its fine structure is its own operational resolution (and its
  deliberate label-distortion is characterized, not canonical) — it registers *onto* the datum, it does not
  *define* it.
- The **datum/projection** is **not** the realization's fidelity. It is the stable *address* (the 2D coarse
  backbone); v9 is the accurate *placer*. Do not conflate the coordinate with the placement.
- None of this is a **new discovery**. It is a reference *standard*. The discovery is the κ/E-series program
  it grounds; this makes that science comparable across instruments, versions, labs, and time.

## How it wires with live v9 (additive registration, zero deployment risk)

The wiring is **not** replacement — v9's placement path is untouched. It is *registration*:

1. v9 places a genome exactly as today (lineage + calibrated confidence).
2. **Additionally**, v9 emits the **datum-canonical angular coordinate θ** — its angle transformed into the
   anchored frame (E. coli = 0) via the **published transform** (`datum_transform_v9.json`: 2D backbone basis
   + anchor gauge + measured precision). This is the shared, certifiable-candidate axis (16.8° cross-instrument).
3. The **radial coordinate is emitted as ADVISORY** (labeled, not certified — Spearman 0.46).
4. v9's structure *beyond* the coarse 2D backbone is **v9's own operational resolution**, not a residual to
   reconcile. The fine-scale codec↔v9 divergence is *mostly the atlas resolving real genus structure the
   dim-16 codec is too coarse to hold* — evidenced (leaf-contrastive raises **held-out** novel-species genus
   accuracy 0.41→0.51; arbitrary distortion could not generalize). So the codec holds the **coarse canonical
   backbone** (witness); v9 does the **fine resolution** (operational); they *should* diverge at fine scale.
   **Open:** whether that resolution is metric-faithful or discriminatively-warped is untested — needs an
   evolutionary-distance (patristic) anchor we don't have. Do not narrate the fine residual as "the functional
   overlay"; that is a *further* claim, not what this measures.

**The transform is candidate, not certified** — scope is the θ backbone only (`datum_transform_v9.json` marks
`certified: false`, freeze-gate = angular median < ~10° and determinate-quartet agreement ≥ 0.90). It is a
published mechanism ready to wire *once precision is certified*, not a live deployment.

**Why this is the point:** once v9's output carries a datum-registered θ, its coordinates become comparable
*through the datum*, across labs and time. **One caveat, corrected this iteration:** cross-*version*
comparability (v9↔v10.6) is a *weak* test, because v10 warm-starts from v9 — the agreement is largely
*inheritance*, not independent rediscovery. The load-bearing invariance test is the **codec**, an independent
lineage (different dimension, different objective), which is why it — not cross-version — is what certifies the
frame is real and not a v9 artifact. Cross-version is demoted from "the reason a datum exists" to "a
convenience that rides on shared ancestry."

## What it would take to certify (candidate → frozen)

Not more grounding hunts. **Precision refinement of the angular axis:**
- Tighten the cross-instrument angular residual (16.8° median → target, e.g., <10° / 90% within 30°).
- Over-determine the anchor gauge (more than 2 anchors → a more stable prime meridian).
- Reach the 0.90 topology-agreement tolerance on determinate quartets.
These are scale/anchoring/alignment work on a frame we've shown is real — not a search for a new ruler.

- Do **not** sell the codec as a discovery. It is infrastructure.

- Do **not** conflate the datum (the 2D *coordinate*) with the realization's *fidelity* (which lives in

  those are the purity tests passing.
